# Feature to benefit

Feature (a fact) -> Advantage (what the fact does) -> Benefit (what the buyer experiences).

Buyers purchase the benefit. The advantage is the joint between the fact and the benefit: leave it out and the buyer has no idea what your number means for them.

## How to fill the three layers

Using a microfibre pillow as the example:

| Layer | Content | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feature | 0.8 denier microfibre | The fact in the product documentation |
| Advantage | The fibre is so fine that a kilometre of it weighs 0.8 g, so the same volume packs in more fibres and more contact points | The physical consequence of the fact |
| Benefit | Your ear does not get hot and stuffy when you sleep on your side | Something the buyer can feel |

Working backwards is often faster: start with how a buyer would describe the product to a friend, then find the fact that supports it.

## How to tell you got it right

- Read the benefit on its own. If the buyer cannot picture it immediately, you are still on the advantage layer.
  - Empty: improved comfort / more durable / more professional
  - Concrete: two hours of dancing and the soles of your feet are still soft / a week of travel without a charger
- Every benefit must trace back to a specific fact. If you cannot name the fact, the benefit is invented.
- Facts must be checkable, and numbers come first: 30,000 open-close cycles, 30 minutes to 80%, 72 hours of salt spray.
- One fact can produce several benefits (the same fine fibre: no pressure on the ear, no trapped sweat, no clumping after washing), and one benefit can rest on several facts.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Example | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feature only | "Uses PCM phase-change material" | Add the advantage and benefit; if you cannot, delete it |
| Adjectives only | "High-quality fabric, comfortable and breathable" | Replace with weight per square metre, air permeability, measured temperature difference |
| Benefit too abstract | "Improves sleep quality" | Replace with a scene the buyer has lived through |
| A fake benefit that flatters the seller | "German engineering" | Buyers do not pay for process, they pay for what the process delivers |
| Numbers with no reason | Fifteen figures crammed into the bullets | Keep the ones that support a benefit, move the rest to the description or A+ |

## Questions to ask

Ask in this order when you need facts from the product side:

1. How was this figure measured? Is there a test report or a photo?
2. How does it differ from the usual approach in this category, and by how much?
3. What do buyers complain about most, and how do we solve it?
4. Do you have return reasons, negative reviews, or support tickets? These are the best source of pain points.
5. Where and how often is this used, and alongside what?
6. If the buyer could remember only one thing, what should it be?
